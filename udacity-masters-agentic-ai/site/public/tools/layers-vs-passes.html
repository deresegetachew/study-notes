01

A layer

One fixed block of computation — attention, then a small per-position network. Every model has a set number, wired at training time. It never changes based on the prompt.

02

A pass

One full trip through every layer, for one token. Direct answers take a couple of passes; chain-of-thought takes as many as the reasoning is long.

03

Why CoT runs longer

Not because the network works differently — because "step by step" text makes EOS look unlikely for longer, so the ordinary decoding loop just keeps going.

Why "20" — and why EOS waits

Neither choice is computed. Both are predicted, because patterns like these appear constantly across the model's training data:

Terse Q&A pattern 
Q: What is 17 × 24?
A: 408 ← EOS looks likely right here
Worked-example pattern 
Let's think step by step.
17 × 20 = 340.
17 × 4 = 68.
340 + 68 = 408. ← EOS waits until here
Simplified for intuition: 8 layers, chunked "tokens," and hand-set probabilities to make the shape of the effect visible. Real models have far more layers, split text into subwords, and sample from a full vocabulary distribution — but the mechanism illustrated here (fixed depth per pass, EOS as an ordinary predicted token, passes driven by the decoding loop) is exactly how it works.
