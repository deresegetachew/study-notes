# Topic 1.1: Architectural & Engineering Levers for Model Output Optimization

> Module: Agentic AI · Date: 2026-08-27

**Q: What are the two main levers for optimizing an AI system's output?**
Prompt strategy (how you structure instructions) and model selection (balancing capability, cost, and latency).

**Q: What are the building blocks of a well-structured prompt?**
System prompt (global rules/tone), Role (persona to steer reasoning depth), Constraints (limits, format, boundaries), explicit Reasoning/Planning (e.g. Chain-of-Thought), and the User prompt (runtime task/context). Example: an elite-trainer persona (Role) generating a workout plan bounded by equipment and goals (Constraints).

**Q: What's the "Engineering Trilemma" in model selection?**
Capability, Cost, and Latency compete — you can't max out all three. Bigger models reason better but cost more and respond slower; smaller models are cheap and fast but need more explicit, rigid prompting to hit comparable accuracy.

**Q: How do you get away with using a cheaper model?**
Instead of relying on a large model to infer all steps from a vague prompt, decompose the task yourself — Chain-of-Thought, Prompt Chaining — into simple steps a small/cheap model can execute reliably.

**Remember:** you're trading your own upfront engineering effort (designing the decomposition) for runtime savings (speed + cost) in production — same or better outcome, cheaper model.
